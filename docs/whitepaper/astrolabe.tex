\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Parkinson's symptoms fluctuate hour to hour, but the paper diary intended to
capture that is abandoned within days: in the cohort studied here only
$61.6\%$ of possible hours carry an entry, \emph{with} research staff
supporting the process. We ask whether wrist accelerometry can fill the gaps,
and — more importantly — whether a system can know when it cannot.

We report two results of opposite sign. Tremor presence is recoverable across
people (held-out $\mathrm{AUC}=0.697\pm0.075$). The seven-point subjective
motor scale is not: nine feature sets over five participant-level folds all
fail to beat a constant predictor, and the failure mode is a sign flip in the
feature--state relationship between individuals rather than insufficient
capacity. We therefore do not predict it.

The contribution is the uncertainty machinery around both. Credible intervals
are widened on held-out participants until empirical coverage matches the
nominal level ($0.903$ against a $0.90$ target), abstention is tuned to a fixed
\emph{error budget} rather than a fixed rate, and selective prediction is
measured rather than asserted: accuracy rises from $0.713$ to $0.825$ as the
answered fraction falls to a quarter. Removing one wrist forces abstention from
$12.4\%$ to $77.3\%$ to hold the same budget --- degradation the system
discovers rather than is told.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data}

\textbf{COPS} (Continuous Observation of Parkinsonian Symptoms; Nesser et al.,
CC-BY 4.0) provides 66 participants wearing GENEActiv accelerometers on both
wrists at $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$, paired with hourly self-reported symptom diaries
over roughly a week each. After excluding participants without usable paired
data, $6{,}530$ hours across $65$ participants remain.

\paragraph{The alignment convention.} A diary row stamped $T$ describes the
window $[T-1, T)$. This is stated once in the study documentation and is easy
to invert; doing so shifts every label by one hour and silently degrades every
downstream result. \texttt{io\_cops.py} re-derives the window from the filename
and raises on mismatch, so a misaligned join cannot be constructed.

\paragraph{Cohort caveat.} $46$ of $66$ participants have deep brain
stimulation. This is an advanced, device-treated population; both the movement
signature and the fluctuation pattern differ from newly diagnosed patients, and
nothing here should be assumed to transfer unchanged.

\section{Features}

Each wrist signal is band-passed into the two bands the COPS authors themselves
use: a movement band $0.1\text{--}3\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and a tremor band
$4\text{--}8\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Band power, spectral ratios, and bilateral
asymmetries are computed over $10$-minute windows, giving $122$ features.

Butterworth filters of order $8$ are applied as second-order sections; the
transfer-function form is numerically unstable at this order and returns
\texttt{NaN} silently, which is a failure that propagates invisibly to the
model rather than crashing.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Ordinal emissions}

The kinesia scale is ordinal with $K=7$ levels. Following Frank and Hall we
decompose it into $K-1$ cumulative binary problems, fit a gradient-boosted
classifier to each, and difference the results:
\begin{equation}
\Pr(z = k \mid x) \;=\; \Pr(z > k-1 \mid x) - \Pr(z > k \mid x),
\end{equation}
enforcing monotonicity of the cumulative estimates by a running minimum before
differencing, so no negative mass can appear.

\subsection{Dividing out the prior}

A discriminative classifier returns $p(z \mid x)$, but the state-space model
below requires an emission likelihood $p(x \mid z)$. By Bayes' rule, up to a
factor constant in $z$,
\begin{equation}
p(x \mid z) \;\propto\; \frac{p(z \mid x)}{p(z)} .
\end{equation}
Omitting the division counts the class prior twice --- once in the classifier
and once in the chain --- which biases the posterior toward the majority state
in a way that looks like smoothing working well.

\subsection{Temporal model}

Motor state is persistent, so we place a hidden Markov model over the day and
recover the posterior by forward--backward in log space. The transition matrix
is built as a convex combination rather than a matrix root,
\begin{equation}
T \;=\; (1-s)\,I + s\,B ,
\end{equation}
with $B$ the empirical hour-to-hour transition matrix and switch rate
$s=0.02$. A matrix root of $B$ can contain negative entries and is not a valid
stochastic matrix at fine time steps.

Likelihoods are tempered before smoothing,
\begin{equation}
\tilde\ell(x \mid z) \;=\; \frac{1}{\tau}\,\log p(x \mid z), \qquad \tau = 25,
\end{equation}
selected on a held-out fold. Without tempering the chain is over-confident and
the entire day collapses onto a single state.

\subsection{Calibrated credible intervals}

For posterior $\gamma$ at a step we take the shortest contiguous interval
carrying mass $m$,
\begin{equation}
[\,\ell, u\,] \;=\; \arg\min_{u-\ell} \;\Big\{\, \textstyle\sum_{k=\ell}^{u} \gamma_k \;\ge\; m \,\Big\}.
\end{equation}
Crucially $m$ is \emph{not} set to the nominal $0.90$. Requesting $90\%$ of
posterior mass does not deliver $90\%$ empirical coverage, and asserting
otherwise is precisely the unfalsifiable claim this system exists to avoid.
Instead $m$ is swept on participants held out of \emph{both} the emission fit
and the test fold until achieved coverage reaches the target:
\begin{equation}
m^{\star} \;=\; \min\Big\{ m : \tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i \mathbf{1}\big[\, z_i \in [\ell_i(m), u_i(m)] \,\big] \;\ge\; 0.90 \Big\}.
\end{equation}
This yields $m^{\star}=0.72$ and achieved coverage $0.903$.

\paragraph{A bug worth reporting.} Interval mass was initially fitted on the
raw emission posterior and applied to the smoothed HMM output. The two
distributions have entirely different sharpness, and coverage landed at
$0.535$ against a $0.90$ target --- which the interface would have displayed as
``90\%''. Calibration and inference now share one code path
(\texttt{infer\_posterior}) so the two cannot diverge.

\subsection{Abstention as a fixed error budget}

A step is declined when the posterior is uninformative:
\begin{equation}
\text{abstain} \iff \max_k \gamma_k < \pi \;\;\lor\;\; (u - \ell + 1) \ge w .
\end{equation}
The threshold $\pi$ is \emph{not} tuned to a target abstention rate. Doing so
guarantees that every sensor configuration declines equally often, so a
degraded configuration cannot exhibit degradation --- the threshold simply
absorbs it. We observed exactly this: with one wrist blanked, abstention
\emph{fell} from $12\%$ to $46\%$ in the wrong direction, because
gradient-boosted trees route missing features down a default branch and return
a \emph{sharper} posterior on strictly less evidence.

Instead $\pi$ holds a fixed risk on answered steps. Writing
$\mathcal{A}(\pi)$ for the answered set and $\hat z_i = \gamma_i^\top(0,\dots,6)$,
\begin{equation}
\pi^{\star} \;=\; \min\Big\{ \pi : \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{A}(\pi)|}\textstyle\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}(\pi)} |\hat z_i - z_i| \;\le\; \beta \Big\},
\end{equation}
with budget $\beta$ set by the reference configuration. A worse configuration
can only meet $\beta$ by answering less, so the abstention rate becomes a
consequence of measured accuracy.

\subsection{Why the subjective scale fails}

The cumulative classifiers reveal the mechanism. Held-out AUCs for
$\Pr(z>k)$ are $0.428$, $0.530$, $0.587$, $0.324$, $0.455$ --- three of five
\emph{below chance}. A classifier at $0.5$ has no signal; one at $0.324$ has
signal pointing the wrong way. The feature-to-state mapping inverts between
individuals, and no amount of regularisation repairs a sign that flips.

Personalisation does not rescue it. Given identical hours and the \emph{same}
calibration labels, a participant's own constant scores ordinal MAE $0.400$
while the global model plus a per-participant offset scores $0.474$. The offset
was learning the person's mean; the global mapping it shifted added nothing.

\subsection{The caveat that must be stated}

Pooled out-of-fold AUC is $0.681$, but the median \emph{within-participant} AUC
is $0.550$, above chance on $25$ of $36$ scorable participants. Much of the
pooled figure separates tremor-dominant people from everyone else rather than a
tremulous hour from a calm one \emph{in the same person} --- and within-person
discrimination is what a diary requires. Both figures ship in the output
contract.

\section{What the system will not claim}

The seven-point motor scale is never inferred. It is displayed as reported by
the patient, or as an explicit abstention. On the demonstration
participant-day, the model declines all $114$ steps, its peak posterior never
exceeding $0.549$ against a threshold of $0.58$ fixed on other participants.
That is the system behaving correctly.

No causal claim is made. Trajectory shape around recorded medication times is
temporal alignment, not treatment response; establishing response requires a
controlled protocol. This is not a medical device.
